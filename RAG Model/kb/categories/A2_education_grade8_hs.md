A2 covers students from Grade 8 through high school graduation. The appropriate computer is a Tier 2 (Standard) device because high school workloads include essay writing in word processors, slide preparation, light coding in introductory CS classes, and the occasional creative project in image editing tools. Grade 7 students are assigned to A2 by default in the MVP — pending stakeholder confirmation.

## Who qualifies

- Students between Grade 8 and Grade 12, or the equivalent age range in alternative-education programs.
- Newcomer teens enrolled in ESL streams within secondary schools also fit here (the matching engine may still split them into [A2, F]).

## Typical use cases

- Writing essays in Word, Google Docs, or Pages.
- Slide preparation in PowerPoint, Google Slides, or Keynote.
- Introductory programming in Python or web tech — VS Code with a handful of extensions runs cleanly on T2.
- Online research, video calls, and digital portfolios.
- Light photo or audio editing for school projects.

## Tier rationale

T2 is the right answer for the vast majority of A2 cases. T3 is acceptable as a fallback if no T2 is available, but the student will hit ceilings sooner. T1 is overkill — those machines should be reserved for software-engineering post-secondary students or video-editing professionals.

## Edge cases

- Students enrolled in IB Computer Science or AP CS using Java in IntelliJ: T2 is still adequate.
- Students doing serious video editing for a high school media class (DaVinci Resolve, Premiere): bump to T1 only if the program/teacher confirms.
- Students sharing one device among siblings: flag for staff to consider a second device bundle.
