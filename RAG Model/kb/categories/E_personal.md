E covers personal browsing — the applicant needs a device for everyday internet use that does not fall under any other category. Per the direct-linking rules, E receives a Tier 3 (Basic) device exclusively. Nothing more powerful is appropriate.

## Who qualifies

- Adults who are not currently working, studying, applying for a job, or managing healthcare.
- People reconnecting with technology after a long gap.
- Anyone whose stated use is "staying connected" or "browsing".

## Typical use cases

- Email, social media, news, video streaming.
- Light document work — filling out forms, writing letters, printing.
- Video calls with family or friends.

## Tier rationale

The use case is universally web- and document-driven. T3 covers it. Allocating a T2 or T1 here wastes the device on a workload it does not need, and starves a higher-need applicant. The MVP does not encode category-vs-category priority weighting; this restraint encodes it implicitly via the efficiency score.

## Edge cases

- Applicants who later disclose a secondary use (job search, education) should be re-categorized via the splitter rather than upgraded within E.
